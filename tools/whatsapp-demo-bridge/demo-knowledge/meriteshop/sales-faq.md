# Merit Cables — FAQ

## What's the difference between a wire and a cable?
A wire is a single conductor, while a cable is a bundle of two or more insulated wires wrapped together.

## How do I choose the right wire size?
It depends on the current (amperage) and voltage of what you're powering, measured via the cross-sectional area in mm². Merit Cables doesn't publish a fixed size-to-load table — for a precise recommendation, use their Cable Size Calculator or contact the Merit Cables team for a load calculation.

## What does mm² mean?
Square millimeters — the cross-sectional area of a wire's conductor.

## Stranded vs. solid wire?
Solid wires suit permanent fixed installations; stranded wires are more flexible, better for applications with movement.

## What is wire insulation for?
It protects the conductor from damage and moisture, and prevents electrical hazards. Common materials: PVC, rubber, XLPE.

## Can indoor cable be used outdoors?
Not recommended — outdoor use needs cable specifically rated for outdoor/environmental exposure.

## What do the wire colors mean?
Brown/red = live, blue/black = neutral, green/yellow striped = earth/ground.

## What are shielded cables for?
They have a conductive layer that protects against electromagnetic interference — used in high-electrical-noise environments.

## Does temperature affect cable performance?
Yes — high heat degrades insulation over time, extreme cold can make it brittle. Match the cable's rated temperature range to where it's installed.

## What is voltage drop?
The reduction in voltage as current travels through a conductor — minimized with correctly sized cable and shorter cable runs.

## Is it safe to mix different cable types in one installation?
Generally not advisable — risk of incompatibility, safety issues, and code violations.

## BS2004 vs BS6004?
Both are British Standards for low-voltage PVC cables; BS6004 specifically also covers flexible cable specifications.

## Ethernet/data cable categories?
Cat5e supports up to 1 Gbps; Cat6 supports up to 10 Gbps over shorter distances; Cat6a supports 10 Gbps over up to 100 meters.

## How should cable be stored?
In a cool, dry place, avoiding sharp bends, with regular inspection for damage.
