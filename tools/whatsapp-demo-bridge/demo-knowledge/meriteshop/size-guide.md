# Merit Cables — Size Guide & Cable Selection

## Important — no fixed size/load table exists on the website
The Merit Cables Size Guide page does NOT publish a table matching cable size to specific loads, house sizes, or appliances. It only provides a glossary of electrical terms and points customers to an external Cable Size Calculator (cablesizecalculator.com) or to calling Merit Cables directly for a load calculation: "Ethical Customer support - Call us to find out your load and recommended Cables & Wires."

Because of this, never confidently state "use X mm² for your house/appliance" from general knowledge alone. When a customer asks what size cable they need:
1. Ask a clarifying question first — application (house wiring / appliance / solar / industrial), approximate load or number of rooms/marla if known, single-phase or three-phase if relevant, and cable run length.
2. If the individual product descriptions in the catalog mention a specific recommendation (e.g. a product page saying "recommended for up to 7 Marla house wiring"), you may share that specific product's own stated guidance.
3. For anything beyond what a product page or this knowledge base actually states, be honest that a precise recommendation needs a proper load calculation — point them to the Cable Size Calculator or to a Merit Cables team member / a qualified electrician, rather than guessing.

## Electrical glossary (from the Merit Cables Size Guide page)
- Ampere (A): the unit of electrical current.
- AC/DC: Alternating Current / Direct Current.
- mm² (square millimeters): the cross-sectional area of a wire's conductor — larger mm² generally means the cable can safely carry more current.
- Resistance / conductivity: how much a material resists or allows current flow; copper is a common low-resistance conductor.

## Wire vs. cable, and construction basics (from the Merit Cables FAQ)
- A wire is a single conductor; a cable is a bundle of two or more insulated wires wrapped together.
- Stranded wires (multiple thin strands) are more flexible, suited to movement-heavy applications; solid wires suit permanent fixed installations.
- Insulation (commonly PVC, rubber, or XLPE) protects the conductor from damage, moisture, and electrical hazards — outdoor use needs cable rated for outdoor/environmental exposure; indoor-rated cable should not be used outdoors.
- Standard cable color coding: brown/red for live, blue/black for neutral, green/yellow striped for earth/ground.
- Voltage drop is the reduction in voltage along a conductor — minimized by correctly sizing the cable and keeping cable runs short.
- BS2004 and BS6004 are both British Standards for low-voltage PVC cables; BS6004 specifically covers flexible cable specifications.
